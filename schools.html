The challenge

Where admissions quietly slip away

Parents and students are shortlisting several institutions at once. The decision often comes down to which one replied clearly, followed up on time and made the next step easy.

Enquiries spread across channels

Website forms, email, phone, WhatsApp, walk-ins, education fairs and ad campaigns. Each lands somewhere different, so it's hard to see what needs attention.

Admission season overloads the team

Enquiries peak over a few weeks. The same small team handles calls, visits and paperwork, and some enquiries simply wait too long.

The same questions, hundreds of times

Fees, transport, curriculum, age criteria, documents, deadlines, hostel, scholarships. Staff time goes on repeat answers instead of counselling serious applicants.

Handoffs drop the ball

Front desk to counsellor to admissions office to accounts. When nobody clearly owns the next step, a ready-to-join family hears nothing.

No clear view of the funnel

How many enquiries became visits, then applications, then admissions, by programme or grade? Finding out usually means a manual Excel exercise.

Marketing spend you can't trace

Newspaper ads, social campaigns, fairs, hoardings, referrals. Which ones actually lead to admissions, and which only fill the inbox?

The workflow

A clearer process, from first contact to next step

Schools, colleges and education groups each have their own programmes, stages and handoffs. A useful system should reflect your steps instead of forcing your team into a generic one.

  1. 01

    Enquiry received

    From a form, WhatsApp, call, walk-in or fair, captured in one place.

  2. 02

    Programme interest noted

    Grade or programme, intake year, campus and key questions recorded.

  3. 03

    Team member assigned

    The right counsellor owns it and gets notified right away.

  4. 04

    Follow-up tracked

    Calls, visits, reminders and document requests, logged against the student.

  5. 05

    Application progress reviewed

    Admissions heads see every stage and step in where it's needed.

Straight answers

Questions principals & trustees usually ask

We already have a school ERP. Why would we need this?

Many ERPs are built for enrolled students (attendance, fees, exams). The enquiry-to-admission stage before that is often handled in registers and spreadsheets. We can focus on that gap and look at how it connects to the ERP you already use, without replacing it.

What if the chatbot gives parents wrong information?

The chatbot answers from information your admissions team provides and approves, covering fees, documents, timings and eligibility. Anything outside that, or anything sensitive, is handed to a staff member instead of guessed.

Our admissions team isn't very technical. Will they use it?

That's exactly why we build around how they already work. Simple screens, only the fields you need and WhatsApp built into the workflow. We train staff at rollout and stay available afterwards.

Can it be ready before our admission season?

It depends on scope and how close the season is. That's why we recommend a phased approach: get the core enquiry pipeline live first, then add the chatbot, automation and reporting. You'll get a realistic timeline with the scoped proposal, and the earlier we start, the better.

What does it cost?

The consultation is free. Cost depends on what you need, from a simple enquiry tracker with reminders to a full admissions CRM with chatbot and integrations. After mapping your process, you get a clear written scope and quote before committing.

How is student and parent data protected?

Role-based access is part of the design, so counsellors see their enquiries and leadership sees the full picture. Hosting, access rights and data handling are agreed with you during scoping to match your institution's policies.
